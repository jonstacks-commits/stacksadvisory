Enterprise Account Strategy · Multi-Stakeholder Accounts

Synergy targets are only real once three sales teams agree on who owns the account.

Every M&A revenue-synergy case assumes a unified go-to-market motion the day after close. In practice, legacy sales reps, newly-acquired specialized teams, and channel partners are often still calling on the same global accounts with different pricing, different messaging, and no shared account plan — and the synergy case quietly erodes.

The commercial question every acquirer eventually asks: who actually owns this account now, and how do we get three sales organizations pulling toward one number?

The Consultancy View

"Commercial synergies call for product, pricing, and sales teams working in lockstep."

McKinsey's M&A practice is explicit that revenue synergies — unlike cost synergies, which can be captured with a spreadsheet exercise — depend on cross-functional coordination across the exact seams where deals break down: legacy versus acquired sales forces, direct versus channel coverage, and pricing that was never built to be reconciled.

Their research also points to the fix: a dedicated integration management office with named owners and measurable KPIs for each synergy lever, rather than leaving commercial integration to "figure itself out" after close. The deals that hit their revenue synergy targets are the ones that treated the sales org redesign as seriously as the deal itself.

Jeff Rudnicki, Paul Küderli, Sebastian Muehlbauer & Gordian Hoffmann — McKinsey & Company, Feb. 2026
The Pattern

Three sales motions, one global account, no shared plan.

The specific brands and territories change deal to deal — the structural collision between legacy, specialized, and channel coverage on the same enterprise account doesn't.

A generalized post-merger commercial integration pattern — not tied to any specific deal, account, or organization.

Why It Erodes

The revenue synergy case fails at the account level, not the model level.

The deal thesis is sound. The failure mode is almost always structural, not strategic.

Overlapping Coverage

Legacy and acquired reps calling on the same buyer with no coordination, eroding trust before the synergy story is even told.

Conflicting Account Ownership

No named owner for the combined relationship means every escalation becomes a turf negotiation instead of a customer conversation.

Inconsistent Messaging

Different pricing, different value props, sometimes in the same week — the customer notices before leadership does.

No Shared Account Plan

Without one plan and one set of KPIs, cross-sell targets stay aspirational instead of becoming quota.

Where It Applies

Four settings, one integration discipline.

The org charts differ; the fix is the same set of decisions, made early and made explicit.

Post-Merger Integration

Legacy & Acquired Sales Force Consolidation

Two sales organizations, two CRMs, two comp plans, one set of global accounts — and a synergy target on the board deck that assumes it already works.

Commercial angle: a named integration owner and a reconciled account plan convert a synergy assumption into a trackable KPI within the first two quarters.

Multi-Business-Unit Accounts

Global Pharma Account Coordination

Large pharma accounts spanning multiple product lines and business units, each with its own rep and its own view of the relationship.

Commercial angle: a single strategic account framework surfaces cross-BU opportunity that no individual rep can see alone.

Channel Coexistence

Direct & Channel Overlap Resolution

Newly acquired channel relationships colliding with existing direct coverage on the same named accounts — a common step-out-deal byproduct.

Commercial angle: clear rules of engagement protect channel margin and direct relationships simultaneously, instead of forcing a zero-sum fight.

Revenue Synergy Enablement

Cross-Sell Across Newly Combined Portfolios

The combined portfolio has genuine cross-sell logic on paper — but the reps who'd need to pitch it have never been trained on, or compensated for, the other company's product line.

Commercial angle: enablement and comp plan redesign turn a synergy slide into pipeline within the first sales cycle post-close.
