Sales Methodology · Structured Qualification for Complex Deals

One is a map. The other is a scanner.

Miller Heiman's Strategic Selling and LAMP tradition and MEDDPICC get debated as if a sales org has to pick one. They don't compete — they solve different problems at different altitudes. One designs how a long-cycle, multi-stakeholder pursuit gets won. The other tells you, with evidence, whether the deal in front of you is actually healthy.

Run only the map and you get eloquent strategy that fails inspection. Run only the scanner and you get immaculate inspection with no strategy behind it. De-risking enterprise pursuits means running both, deliberately, at the same time.

8 Pillars
Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Identified Pain, Champion, Competition — each evidenced in a CRM note, an email, or a dated plan.
1 Objective
Strategic Selling's discipline of forcing a Single Sales Objective for every pursuit — before templates, before forms.
The Practitioner View

"One is a map; the other is a kind of scanner. One points the team in the right direction; the other tells you whether the deal is healthy enough to run."

After two decades leading enterprise sales organizations through cycles of increasing complexity, this practitioner's conclusion is that the debate over which methodology is "better" invites the wrong behavior entirely. Strategic Selling gives a team the strategic map — relationship mapping, a named Economic Buyer, a forced Single Sales Objective. MEDDPICC gives the diagnostic spine — inspectable, binary, hard to fake.

The failure mode he names directly: using MEDDPICC as a compliance checklist gets you the form without the force — every letter filled in, the deal still weak underneath it. The combination is what actually moves a forecast number reliably, quarter after quarter.

Matthias Köhler, Enterprise Sales & Leadership — LinkedIn, October 2025
The Two Disciplines

Strategy sets the direction. Qualification tells you if it's real.

Neither framework alone accounts for how a complex, multi-stakeholder enterprise deal actually gets won. Run together, they cover the full altitude — from executive relationship strategy down to the specific, dated evidence that a deal is progressing.

The Map

Strategic Selling / LAMP

  • Map buying influences and name the true Economic Buyer
  • Separate technical objections from political ones
  • Force a Single Sales Objective for every pursuit
  • Align customer outcomes with personal wins for key stakeholders
  • Build a teachable review cadence around relationship and account strategy

Answers: how do we design the pursuit and win the room?

The Scanner

MEDDPICC

  • Metrics — quantified outcomes the customer will recognize as success
  • Economic Buyer & Decision Criteria — named, written, verified
  • Decision Process & Paper Process — the calendar, gates, and path from proposal to PO
  • Identified Pain — measured, not poetic
  • Champion & Competition — power confirmed, position known

Answers: is this deal actually healthy enough to forecast?

Why It Matters

What a hybrid approach actually de-risks.

Honest Forecasting

Inspectable qualification criteria replace stage-percentage debates with evidence — a fifteen-minute review can surface the one real blocker.

Paper Process as Deal Design

Treating the path to signature — proposal to MSA to PO — as part of the deal strategy from day one, not a legal afterthought.

Champion Cultivation

The strategic layer teaches how a coach becomes a true Champion — something a qualification checklist alone can't build.

Coaching Rhythm

A shared language across both frameworks gives managers a consistent, teachable cadence instead of ad hoc deal reviews.

Complex deals need a map and a scanner — not a choice between them.
