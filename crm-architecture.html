The Core Challenge

Most life sciences CRM projects
solve the wrong problem.

They configure the tool. They don't redesign the motion. The result is a system that technically works but doesn't change how deals move, how forecasts are built, or how leadership sees the business.

Data Integrity Without MDM Discipline
Duplicate records, inconsistent field definitions, and no master data governance mean every report starts from a compromised foundation. AI doesn't fix this — it amplifies it.
Manual Quote-to-Cash Creates Version-Control Failures
When pricing, quoting, and booking live in different systems — or spreadsheets — the gap between CRM opportunity value and ERP order value widens every quarter.
Stage Definitions Without Exit Criteria Destroy Forecast Accuracy
If "Propose" means different things to different reps, your weighted pipeline is noise. Forecast calls become storytelling sessions, not data-driven conversations.
No Post-Sale Visibility Breaks the Revenue Loop
When Sales and Customer Success operate in separate systems with no closed-loop feedback, you can't attribute revenue to pipeline, can't measure churn signals early, and can't tie marketing ROI to bookings.
"AI becomes valuable only when the commercial data foundation is stable, governed, and trusted."
If the CRM and ERP aren't aligned, AI doesn't solve the problem — it scales the noise. The sequence matters: establish MDM discipline first, apply automation to repeatable decisions second, and only then introduce predictive intelligence into pipeline scoring, forecast confidence, and pricing guidance.

AI is most valuable when it makes revenue decisions faster, not when it replaces judgment.
1
MDM Discipline — clean, governed, trusted data foundation

Weighted pipeline values (Amount × Stage %) aggregate automatically by rep, region, and product family — providing consistent forecast health visibility across the commercial organization.

Proof Case — Genomics Tools Company

A full Salesforce RevOps build
for a growth-stage genomics platform.

The following engagement (anonymized) demonstrates the full CRM-to-Revenue architecture in a Salesforce + Epicor + HubSpot environment at a genomics tools company at commercial inflection.

The Situation
Fragmented lead-to-revenue stack with no unified system of record
HubSpot managing marketing leads with no structured MQL→SQL handoff to Salesforce
Salesforce opportunity stages undefined — "Propose" meant different things to different reps
No CPQ — pricing and discounting managed via email and spreadsheet
Salesforce and Epicor ERP not synced — opportunity values routinely diverged from order values
No executive dashboard — QBR prep consumed 2–3 days of analyst time per quarter
Post-sale handoff undocumented — CS and Sales operated with no shared visibility
The Build
7-layer commercial operations architecture designed and implemented
HubSpot behavioral + firmographic lead scoring → MQL threshold → Salesforce Lead object → territory auto-routing
6-stage sales model with weighted probabilities, exit criteria, and 32 field-validation rules enforced at each stage
Salesforce CPQ deployed with tiered discount schema (Direct / Partner / OEM), Deal Desk approval flows, and DocuSign integration
Salesforce ↔ Epicor daily middleware sync via Workato — opportunity ID writeback, bookings reconciliation, exception logging
Tableau CRM dashboards: weighted pipeline by rep/geo/product, margin visibility, FX-adjusted revenue, AOP attainment
Quarterly CRM NPS feedback loop (Sales + CS + Marketing) → categorized into UX / Data / Reporting → roadmap prioritization
95% Pipeline completeness target — driven by Salesforce validation rules and stage hygiene
+10% QoQ forecast accuracy improvement target — weighted pipeline governance
−15% Stage velocity reduction target — standardized exit criteria and deal desk automation

The functional split holds regardless of brand: CRM governs engagement and forecast; ERP governs the transaction; EDW is the finance-owned system no other function is allowed to compromise — because it's what feeds investors.

The customer journey the whole stack exists to support maps cleanly onto it too: Find and Decide live in marketing and the web; Buy spans web, customer service, and cart checkout; Receive is inventory and fulfillment; Use touches R&D, manufacturing, and sales analytics; Support closes the loop post-sale. Every one of those stages writes to, or reads from, the same three-system backbone.
