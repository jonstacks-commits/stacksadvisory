Oncology Diagnostics Practice · Liquid Biopsy Taxonomy

Liquid biopsy isn't one market.
It's four — and they don't share a playbook.

Minimal residual disease, multi-cancer early detection, treatment response monitoring, and comprehensive genomic profiling get pitched as one category because they share a blood draw. They don't share an evidence bar, a payer conversation, or a competitive set. We use a four-category taxonomy — built on the open-data category work at OpenOnco — to keep commercial strategy honest about which job a given test is actually doing.

The Taxonomy

Four categories, one evidence curve.

Comprehensive genomic profiling sits on twenty years of tissue-liquid concordance data. MCED is asking a mortality-reduction question that won't have a clean answer for years. Positioning a test as if it sits somewhere it doesn't — in either direction — is the fastest way to lose a payer or a KOL relationship you'll need again.

CGP
Comprehensive Genomic Profiling
Clinical question
What actionable alterations does this tumor carry right now?
Evidence stage
Established — validated against tissue NGS across multi-year, real-world data sets.
Reimbursement
Broadest of the four. Medicare LCDs and most commercial payers cover liquid CGP for advanced solid tumors.
Commercial angle
Closest to commodity. Differentiate on turnaround time, tissue-liquid concordance, and panel breadth — not on proving the use case exists.
MRD
Minimal Residual Disease
Clinical question
After curative-intent treatment, is there detectable disease left — and should adjuvant therapy escalate or de-escalate?
Evidence stage
Fast-moving — the most active prospective trials reading out right now across colorectal, breast, and lung.
Reimbursement
The fastest-growing reimbursement story in liquid biopsy. Medicare and major payers have moved in several tumor types within two years.
Commercial angle
The near-term opportunity. Build the motion around named trial readouts and guideline-inclusion timing — not general awareness.
TRM
Treatment Response Monitoring
Clinical question
Is ctDNA clearing, plateauing, or rising on therapy — before imaging would show it?
Evidence stage
Maturing — strong retrospective correlation with survival, fewer prospective trials tying a ctDNA-triggered switch to benefit.
Reimbursement
Inconsistent. Covered in some indications through companion diagnostic pathways; out-of-pocket or trial-embedded in most others.
Commercial angle
Separate "tracks with outcome" from "acting on this changes outcome." Payers are asking the second question — get ahead of it.
MCED
Multi-Cancer Early Detection
Clinical question
Is there a cancer signal in an asymptomatic person, and where did it originate?
Evidence stage
Earliest. Screening-population sensitivity and specificity exists; mortality-reduction data is still years out.
Reimbursement
Essentially none at scale. Out-of-pocket today. USPSTF and guideline-body decisions are the gating event.
Commercial angle
The longest runway and highest ceiling. Overselling the evidence bar to a payer or KOL costs credibility on the next conversation.

Where This Goes Wrong
Same chemistry, four different conversations.
The four categories often share the underlying assay chemistry, which is exactly why they get sold as a continuum of one technology. Commercially, treating them as a single story is the most common mistake we see from platforms crossing from one category into another.
A tumor-informed MRD assay walking into a payer meeting with MCED-grade evidence gets rejected for asking too much, too soon. An MCED platform pitching like an MRD test gets rejected for the opposite reason — the audience expected proof that isn't there yet, and the pitch didn't fit the maturity stage it was actually standing on.

Position each test at its actual evidence stage.

A commercial translation model for diagnostics platforms building payer and KOL strategy against the right bar for their category — not the one next door.
