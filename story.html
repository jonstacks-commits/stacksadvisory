The Method

We teach commercial transformation
through a story you already know.

The same six-step logic that gets a leash-free dog park approved by a city council gets a life sciences platform from zero to its first five enterprise accounts. The players are different. The process is identical.

🐕 The Dog Park
Step 1 — Define the Problem
Dogs need off-leash space. Current parks prohibit it. No existing venue meets community needs.
Step 2 — Map the Buyers
City council votes. Parks dept. controls land. Neighbors can block or enable. Dog owners are the user base.
Step 3 — Build the Case
Petition signatures. Usage data from comparable parks. Safety record. Cost-to-city analysis.
Step 4 — Find the Channel
Council agenda process. District 5 rep as champion. Parks board approval pathway. Public comment window.
Step 5 — Execute the Ask
Present at council meeting. Pilot proposal: 90-day trial at Crestmoor. Defined success metrics.
Step 6 — Measure & Sustain
Usage logs. Incident tracking. Expansion vote at 90 days. Playbook for next park.

🧬 Life Sciences Commercial Build
Step 1 — Diagnose the Commercial Gap
No pipeline visibility. Founder-led sales hitting a ceiling. ICP undefined. Data fragmented across silos.
Step 2 — Map the Buyers
Who in biopharma/CGT decides. Who champions platform adoption. Procurement vs. scientific vs. ops buyers.
Step 3 — Build the Commercial Case
Competitive landscape. TAM/SAM/SOM. Scientific-to-commercial messaging. ROI calculator for the buyer.
Step 4 — Activate the Channel
CRO/CDMO ecosystem. Direct enterprise outreach. Partner-enabled pipeline. CRM built for your motion.
Step 5 — Execute the Pioneer Program
First 5 anchor accounts identified, engaged, and closed. LOIs. Proof structure for the next 50.
Step 6 — Embed & Scale
Reporting layer. Quota model. Playbook your team owns. The system that keeps working after we're done.
The Process

Six steps from
zero to commercial momentum.

Each step is a defined phase with specific inputs, activities, and deliverables. No ambiguity about what happens, in what order, or what you own at the end.

1
Diagnose
Map the current state — including your specific environment.
Before any strategy, we need to understand what you're actually working with. Not just the commercial gaps — the technical reality. Are your contacts in Salesforce, HubSpot, or a spreadsheet? Is your sequencing data coming off an Illumina NovaSeq, a PacBio, or Nanopore? What LIMS are you running, if any? These aren't footnotes. They define every downstream decision.
Commercial infrastructure audit Tech stack mapping Pipeline quality review ICP definition
Dog park equivalent: Walking the current parks, counting dogs, photographing conditions, and talking to the neighbors who already let their dogs run off-leash anyway. You learn the actual situation before you write the proposal.
2
Design
Build the commercial thesis and define the target accounts by name.
Not "biopharma companies in the US." Named organizations, named decision-makers, named buying contexts. The commercial thesis is the argument for why your platform wins — in the language of the buyer who signs the contract, not the scientist who uses it. This is where scientific credibility becomes commercial currency.
GTM thesis Named account prioritization Scientific-to-commercial messaging Competitive landscape
Dog park equivalent: Identifying that Crestmoor Park is the right site, that Council Member Sawyer is the path to yes, and that the argument is community health + property values — not just "dogs need to run."
3
Validate
Pressure-test the strategy before full execution.
Pilot outreach with 3–5 accounts. Early partner conversations. Market signal collection. The goal is to find out where the thesis breaks before you've committed a full quarter to it. What objections surface? Which personas respond? Which channel creates friction? This is where strategy meets reality — cheaply, before scale.
Pilot outreach sequences Early discovery calls Objection mapping Partner discovery
Dog park equivalent: Informally testing the proposal at a neighborhood association meeting before the formal council presentation. Finding out that three neighbors are concerned about fencing before it becomes a public objection.
4
Launch
Execute the Pioneer Program and activate the commercial infrastructure.
This is the execution phase — and execution means specific things. BD outreach campaigns running. CRM operational with real pipeline data in it. Key accounts engaged, not just identified. LOIs in process. Partners activated. The infrastructure and the pipeline running simultaneously. This is where most fractional advisors hand off a deck. We're still in the room.
Pioneer program execution LOI negotiation CRM activation Partner pipeline BD outreach execution
Dog park equivalent: Presenting at the council meeting, managing the Q&A, proposing the 90-day pilot, and coordinating the parks department paperwork — not handing the neighbor a template and wishing them luck.
5
Realize
Close the anchor accounts. Build the proof structure for the next wave.
The Pioneer Program doesn't just create revenue — it creates evidence. Case studies, reference accounts, proof-of-concept structures, and the standardization business case that converts your second-source accounts to sole-source. Every closed deal is engineered to generate the next three.
Account closing Case study development Standardization business case Reference account program
Dog park equivalent: The 90-day pilot goes well. Usage data collected. Incident log is clean. Three council members who voted no are now on-site watching their constituents use the park. The expansion vote is easy.
6
Embed & Scale
Install the system your team owns and runs without us.
The playbook is documented. The CRM is running on your team's rhythm, not ours. The territory model, quota framework, and onboarding plan are in place. The reporting layer gives leadership the visibility to manage the commercial function. We've built something that compounds — not something that collapses when the engagement ends.
Commercial playbook CRM handoff Quota and territory model Team onboarding plan Reporting layer
Dog park equivalent: The Friends of Crestmoor Dog Park is constituted. Maintenance schedule is set. Budget line is in the parks department. The next community that wants a leash-free park can use your playbook.
