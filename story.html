The Commercialization Story

You built the science. Here's how it becomes a business.

Every life sciences company that makes it through commercialization passes through the same six chapters — whether it's a genomics platform's first enterprise logo or a metabolic drug program's first payer conversation. Here's where you are, and here's what comes next.

Day one
You built something real. The science works. The platform works. The early believers are convinced.
Six months in
You're still explaining what you do in every meeting — because there's no repeatable way to say it.
The pattern
No pipeline you'd bet on. No CRM with real data in it. No named path to your next five accounts. Every deal closes by force of personality, not by a system anyone else could run.
The question
Investors keep asking about go-to-market. You keep answering with a plan instead of a motion.
The turn
This isn't a science problem. It's a commercialization problem — and it has a shape. This is where Stacks Advisory enters.

Chapter One · Diagnose

Where growth is actually blocked

You're here if: you have customers, or real interest, but no system that predicts the next one.

Before any strategy, we map what you're actually working with — not just the commercial gaps, but the technical reality underneath them. Your CRM, or the spreadsheet standing in for one. Your data architecture. The pipeline you actually have versus the one in the board deck. This is the chapter where the vague feeling that something is broken gets a name.

Commercial infrastructure auditTech stack mappingPipeline quality reviewICP definition
Chapter Two · Design

Building the commercial engine

You're here if: you know the problem, but don't yet have a plan that survives contact with a real buyer.

Not "biopharma companies in the US" — named organizations, named decision-makers, named buying contexts. This chapter turns your science into an argument a non-scientist signs off on: the plan for how you win customers, the competitive landscape, the account list with names on it.

GTM thesisNamed account prioritizationScientific-to-commercial messagingCompetitive landscape
Chapter Three · Validate

Proving the market will actually buy

You're here if: the plan looks right on paper, but nothing has been tested against a real conversation yet.

Pilot outreach to a handful of accounts. Objection mapping. This is the cheapest chapter to get wrong on purpose — it's far better to find where the plan breaks here than three months into a full launch.

Pilot outreach sequencesEarly discovery callsObjection mappingPartner discovery
Chapter Four · Launch

The revenue engine turns on

You're here if: the plan is validated, and now it has to run at volume — not as a series of one-off favors.

BD outreach live. CRM operational with real pipeline data in it. Key accounts engaged, not just identified. This is the chapter most fractional advisors have already handed you a deck and left. We're still in the room.

Pioneer program executionLOI negotiationCRM activationPartner pipeline
Chapter Five · Realize

Becoming the reference

You're here if: you've closed accounts, but haven't yet turned them into the reason the next five close faster.

Every closed deal should generate the next three. This is where case studies, reference accounts, and the standardization case convert your early wins from proof-of-concept into inevitability.

Account closingCase study developmentStandardization business caseReference account program
Chapter Six · Embed & Scale

The company that runs without you

You're here if: revenue is real, but it still depends entirely on you personally to keep moving.

The playbook is documented. The CRM runs on your team's rhythm, not ours. Territory model, quota framework, and reporting layer are in place — infrastructure that started as separate parts is now connected end to end. That's the point it stops being a set of tools and starts functioning as your commercial operating system: the layer that keeps running, compounding, and adapting after the engagement ends. That's the actual finish line.
